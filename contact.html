What you can write about

You can write to us about any of the following — and anything else to do with the site:

  • General questions — anything about the site, how it works, or what we publish. If you are not sure where a question belongs, send it anyway; it will reach the right person.
  • Recipe requests — tell us the dish, the occasion, or the ingredient you would like a roundup for. The more specific you are, the better: "a roundup of slow cooker soups for January" helps us far more than "more recipes". Reader requests are one of the main ways we choose new topics.
  • Corrections — if you spot a mistake in a recipe, a photograph that shows the wrong dish, or a broken link, please tell us. Include the name of the article and, if you can, the recipe number — it helps us find and fix the problem fast. We correct mistakes promptly and note the correction on the article.
  • Copyright or image concerns — if you believe an image or a piece of text on this site belongs to you, or is used without permission, please tell us. Include the page address and a description of the material. We take these reports seriously and look into them right away.
  • Partnerships — if you would like to work with us, for example on a recipe feature or a seasonal collection, tell us who you are and what you have in mind. We do not currently publish sponsored content, and any future partnership would be disclosed clearly.
  • Business inquiries — any other professional or commercial question about the site. Please include your name, what you do, and the best way to reach you.

What happens after you write

We read every message. Email is the fastest way to reach us — it goes straight to the editors. Messages on Pinterest are read too, though email is better for anything detailed, like a correction with a recipe number or a partnership proposal.

We reply to as many messages as we can, as soon as we can. Corrections and copyright concerns are answered first, because they affect what readers see on the site. Recipe requests are collected and used when we plan new roundups — if your idea becomes an article, that is our reply, and we hope you enjoy cooking from it.

Please do not send the same message more than once. If you have not heard back in a reasonable time, it is fine to send a short follow-up to the same address.

A note on what not to send

To keep yourself safe, please do not send us passwords, payment details, or any other sensitive personal information. We will never ask you for those things by email or on Pinterest, and you do not need them to use this site. If you are reporting a problem, include only the details needed to describe it — the page address, the recipe name, and what went wrong.
