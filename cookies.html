1. What are cookies?

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. When you return to the same site, your browser can send the cookie back, so the site can remember something about your earlier visit. Cookies are a normal part of how the web works, and most websites use them in one form or another.

A cookie on its own is not a program. It cannot run code, and it cannot read files from your device. What it can do is store a small piece of information — for example, a preference you chose, or a random identifier that tells one visit apart from another.

2. Types of cookies

It helps to know the main types, because not all cookies do the same thing:

  • Strictly necessary cookies. These make a website work at all — for example, keeping a page secure or balancing traffic between servers. A site cannot function properly without them, and they do not track you for advertising.
  • Preference cookies. These remember choices you made, such as a language or a layout setting, so you do not have to choose again on your next visit.
  • Analytics cookies. These measure how visitors use a site — which pages are read, how long visits last, and how visitors arrive — so the site owner can improve it.
  • Advertising cookies. These are set by advertising networks to show you ads based on your earlier visits to this and other websites, and to measure whether an ad was seen or clicked.
  • Session cookies disappear when you close your browser. Persistent cookies stay on your device until they expire or you delete them.
  • First-party cookies are set by the site you are visiting. Third-party cookies are set by another domain — for example, an advertising network or a social media service whose button appears on the page.

3. Cookies we use

This website does not set any cookies of its own, and we do not use analytics or advertising cookies. The full picture is:

  • Our cookies: none. We store no preferences in your browser and run no tracking scripts of our own.
  • Hosting (Vercel): our hosting provider may set strictly necessary cookies to deliver pages securely and protect the site from abuse. These cookies cannot be used to identify you personally, and they are not used for advertising.
  • Third-party sites: when you follow a link to another website, such as Pinterest, that site may set its own cookies under its own cookie policy. We do not control those cookies.

4. Why websites use cookies

Websites use cookies for different reasons, and it is worth knowing which ones apply here:

  • To keep the site secure and working — this applies to us, through our hosting provider's strictly necessary cookies.
  • To remember your preferences — this does not apply to us, because we store no preferences.
  • To measure visits — this does not apply to us, because we use no analytics.
  • To show advertising — this does not apply to us, because we show no ads and use no advertising cookies.

5. Cookies from links and buttons

Our pages contain links and buttons that connect to Pinterest — for example, "Follow on Pinterest" and "Save Pin" links on our recipe cards. These do not set cookies on this site. If you click one and arrive on Pinterest, Pinterest may set its own cookies there, under its own cookie policy. The same applies to any other external website you visit through a link on this site.

6. How to manage cookies

You can block or delete cookies at any time in your browser settings. Most browsers let you refuse cookies, clear them after a visit, or allow them only for specific sites. Because this site does not rely on cookies, blocking them should not affect how it works — every recipe and page will still load and read normally.

How to control cookies in your browser:

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Cookies and other site data — block or clear cookies, for all sites or just this one.
  • Safari: Settings (Preferences) → Privacy — block all cookies, or remove stored data website by website.
  • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data — clear data or manage exceptions per site.
  • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Cookies and site data — block third-party cookies or clear what is stored.

Most browsers also let you browse privately (incognito), so cookies are deleted when you close the window. If you want to learn more about cookies in general, the independent guide at allaboutcookies.org explains them clearly.

7. Do Not Track

Some browsers let you send a "Do Not Track" signal with your requests, asking websites not to track you. Because we do not track you in the first place — there are no analytics or advertising tools on this site — there is nothing for such a signal to change here. Your visit is treated the same whether or not the signal is set.

8. Changes to this policy

If we add features that use cookies — for example, analytics or a newsletter — this page will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will change. Significant changes will be reflected here before they take effect where the law requires it.
