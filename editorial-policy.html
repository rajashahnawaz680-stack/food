Editorial Policy

✦

Last updated: November 19, 2026

This page explains how The Tasty Recipe Co creates its content, what standards every article must meet, and how we handle mistakes, images, sponsorships, and AI assistance. If you read our recipes, you deserve to know how they are made — and what we will never do.

In short: We publish original recipe roundups, one article at a time, and check each finished article live. We do not copy recipes, invent facts, or publish sponsored content or affiliate links without clearly saying so.

1. How we create content

We work on one topic at a time. Many topics are screened on Pinterest before an article is written, and some come straight from reader requests and seasonal ideas. Either way, the process is the same: one topic, one article, written carefully and checked live before the next one begins. This keeps our publishing slow, deliberate, and focused on quality.

Every article on this site is a roundup — a collection of dishes built around one occasion, ingredient, or meal. Each recipe in a roundup has its own photograph, a short description, an ingredients list with quantities, and numbered directions with times and temperatures where the dish needs them. The number in an article's title is the number of recipes — and the number of photographs — in that article.

2. Editorial standards

Our descriptions are written in our own words. We do not copy recipes or article text from other sites. Each recipe lists ingredients with quantities and directions with times and temperatures where the dish needs them, written in our own words. We do not publish nutrition figures, and we do not present guesses as facts. If we do not know something, we do not present it as fact.

We write in American English for cooks in the United States, and measurements use US customary units — cups, tablespoons, and Fahrenheit. We aim for a warm, practical tone: useful first, and never fussy.

3. Sourcing

Our roundups are original collections put together by our editors. We do not copy recipes or article text from other sites, and we never imply that we created a recipe that belongs to someone else. Where a dish is a well-known classic — a sugar cookie, a cheese ball, a roast turkey — we write our own version of it in our own words, rather than reproducing someone else's text.

4. Recipe selection

We choose recipes based on usefulness, quality, and variety, as well as seasonality and reader interest. A good roundup needs dishes that home cooks will actually want to make, for the occasion the article promises — not filler to reach a number. We look for a mix of classic and fresh ideas, a range of difficulty, and dishes that work with ingredients a home cook can actually find. Our goal is a collection that helps a home cook find the right dish for the occasion.

5. Fact-checking

Before an article is published, we check dates, measurements, and claims in it. We check that the recipe count matches the number in the title, that every recipe has its own photograph of the right dish, that ingredient quantities are sensible, and that times and temperatures are appropriate for the dish. We do not invent testing, credentials, or reviews, and we do not claim experience we do not have. If a claim cannot be checked, it does not go in.

6. Corrections

Mistakes happen, even with checking. If you spot one — a wrong quantity, a temperature that looks off, a step in the wrong order, a photograph that shows the wrong dish — please report it by emailing us at rajashahnawaz879@gmail.com or messaging us on Pinterest at @thetastyrecipeco. You can also use our Contact page.

We correct mistakes promptly and note the correction on the article, so readers can see what changed. Small typos are fixed silently; anything that affects how a dish turns out gets a visible correction note.

7. Image rights

We use only properly licensed or generated images, with credits where needed. Every recipe in our roundups has its own photograph, and each photograph shows the dish it belongs to — never another recipe's dish. We never download and republish competitor photos, and we never present an image as our own work when it is not.

8. Sponsored content

We do not currently publish sponsored content. If that changes, any sponsored article or placement will be clearly labeled as sponsored here and on the relevant page, before it appears. A sponsor will never be able to buy a favorable review, a recipe recommendation, or a place in a roundup without that being disclosed.

9. Affiliate disclosure

We do not currently use affiliate links. For full details, see our Affiliate Disclosure page. If we ever add affiliate links, we will disclose them there and on the relevant page before any affiliate link appears, and our recommendations will still be chosen on usefulness and quality — never on commissions.

10. AI assistance

AI may assist with drafting, organization, and formatting our content. Every article is reviewed and edited by our team before it is published. AI does not decide what goes into a roundup, and it does not replace the human checks described above — recipe counts, photographs, quantities, and claims are all verified by a person before publication. We do not publish mass-produced spam, and we do not fabricate experiences or quotes.

11. What we never publish

To be specific about our boundaries, we never publish any of the following:

  • Recipes or article text copied from another site.
  • Photographs taken from another site without a license.
  • Fake reviews, fake testimonials, or fabricated cooking experiences.
  • Sponsored content or affiliate links without a clear disclosure.
  • Recipes we have not checked for sensible quantities, times, and temperatures.
  • Content that presents guesses as facts.

12. Contact the editors

Questions about how we work, a correction to report, or a topic you would like us to cover? Email us at rajashahnawaz879@gmail.com or message us on Pinterest at @thetastyrecipeco. You can also see our Contact page.
