About The Tasty Recipe Co

✦

The Tasty Recipe Co is a food blog for home cooks. We publish recipe roundups — collections of dishes built around one occasion, ingredient, or meal — so you can find the right recipe for your table without scrolling through dozens of separate posts. Our goal is simple: to make holiday cooking and everyday meals easier, with ideas worth gathering around.

We are a small, new blog, and we are building it one article at a time. There is no big team behind the site and no flood of rushed posts. Every roundup is chosen, written, and checked by our editors before it goes live — and if it is not good enough to cook from, it does not get published.

Who we are

The Tasty Recipe Co is a food blog that started in November 2026, with a simple idea: a home cook looking for Christmas dinner ideas should not have to open twenty tabs to find them. One good roundup — a full table of dishes, each with its own photograph, ingredients, and directions — is worth more than a hundred scattered links.

Our first article was published on November 13, 2026. Since then we have been adding new roundups every day, starting with the holiday season and building toward everyday cooking. The site is written in American English for cooks in the United States, and measurements use US customary units — cups, tablespoons, and Fahrenheit — so a recipe works the way it is written, without converting anything first.

What we publish

We publish recipe roundups, seasonal food ideas, desserts, and cooking inspiration for home cooks. Our roundups cover six categories:

  • Breakfast & Brunch — slow mornings and weekend brunch tables.
  • Lunch & Dinner — mains for midday and evening tables.
  • Desserts & Baking — cookies, cakes, pies, and sweet bakes.
  • Air Fryer Recipes — lighter, crisp dishes from the air fryer.
  • Healthy Recipes — fresh, light, and wholesome everyday cooking.
  • Appetizers & Snacks — starters, party bites, and snacks for every gathering.

We started with the holiday season — Christmas first, then the New Year — because that is when a good roundup helps most: a crowd to feed, a table to fill, and no time to waste. As the blog grows, we are building toward evergreen recipes for everyday cooking — weeknight dinners, easy lunches, and desserts that work any time of year.

Every article follows the same format. The number in the title is the number of recipes in the article — and the number of photographs, because each recipe has its own picture of the dish itself. Every recipe lists its ingredients with quantities and gives numbered directions with times and temperatures where the dish needs them.

How we work

We work on one topic at a time. Many topics are screened on Pinterest before anything is written, and some come straight from reader requests and seasonal ideas. Either way, the process is the same: one topic, one article, written carefully and checked live before the next one begins.

We would rather publish slowly and carefully than rush out articles that do not meet our standards. A roundup takes time to get right — the recipes have to make sense together, the photographs have to show the right dish, and the quantities, times, and temperatures have to be sensible. If any of that is not right, the article waits until it is.

Our full working method is set out in our Editorial Policy, including how we choose recipes, how we check facts, how we handle corrections, and how we use AI assistance.

How we choose recipes

We choose recipes for our roundups based on usefulness, quality, and variety — dishes that home cooks will actually want to make. A good roundup needs a mix: classics and fresh ideas, easy dishes and ones worth a little more effort, and options for different tastes and diets where the occasion allows.

We also consider seasonality and reader interest, so our collections fit the occasion, the time of year, and the meals people are actually looking for. A Christmas roundup in December should feel like Christmas; a weeknight dinner roundup should be doable on a Tuesday. If a dish does not belong in the collection, it does not go in — even if it would make the list longer.

Images

Every recipe in our roundups has its own photograph, and each photograph shows the dish it belongs to — never another recipe's dish. We use only properly licensed or generated images, with credits where needed. Photos are never presented as our own work when they are not, and we never download and republish competitor photos.

If you ever see a photograph that does not match its recipe, or an image that you believe belongs to you, please tell us — by email or through our Contact page — and we will look into it right away.

Accuracy & corrections

We check dates, measurements, and claims in our articles before publishing, but mistakes can still happen — a typo in a quantity, a temperature that should have been lower, a step in the wrong order. If you spot an error, please let us know by email or through our Contact page, or by messaging us on Pinterest.

We correct mistakes promptly and note the correction on the article, so readers can see what changed. Small typos are fixed silently; anything that affects how a dish turns out gets a visible correction note.

Who writes this site

This site is written by The Tasty Recipe Co Editorial Team — the editors who choose every topic, check every photograph, and write each roundup. We do not publish personal names on this site; the work speaks for the team, and the team stands behind every article. If you have a question for the editors, the way to reach us is by email or on Pinterest, as below.
