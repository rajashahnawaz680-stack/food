Christmas Ham Recipes

A Christmas ham is the kind of main dish that does the work of the whole table: one roast, sliced at the counter, feeds a crowd and leaves the kitchen smelling like the holidays. This roundup gathers twelve ways to cook it — classic sweet glazes, hands-off slow cooker and Instant Pot versions, and bolder flavors for cooks who want something different this year. Pick the one that fits your oven, your schedule, and your table.

How to pick the right ham

Most Christmas hams start as a cured, smoked ham that is already cooked — your job is to warm it through and glaze it, not to cook it from raw. A bone-in ham feeds a crowd and looks generous on the platter; a boneless ham is easier to slice and store. Spiral-sliced hams are the easiest to serve because the slices are already cut, and the glaze seeps between them. Buy by the headcount: a generous slice or two per person, with a little extra if you want leftovers, and check the package for whether the ham is fully cooked or needs cooking. Let the ham rest a few minutes before slicing so the juices settle.

Good to know: Everything is written in American English, and measurements use US customary units — cups, tablespoons, and Fahrenheit — for cooks in the United States. Always follow the package directions and use a food thermometer to confirm the ham is heated through.

Classic glazed hams

These are the hams most people picture when they think of Christmas dinner: sweet, sticky glazes, scored fat, and a dark caramel edge.

1. Brown Sugar Glazed Ham

The classic. A brown sugar glaze melts into the scored fat and turns dark and sticky in the oven, with a little mustard to keep it from tasting one-note. It is the ham to make when you want the table to look and smell exactly like Christmas. Serve it with dinner rolls and something green to cut the sweetness.

2. Honey Glazed Ham

Honey gives a lighter, floral sweetness than brown sugar, and it brushes on easily between the spiral slices. The glaze stays glossy rather than crusty, and the ham slices stay juicy. This is a good pick if your table also has sweet sides like candied yams — the honey keeps the ham from overpowering them.

3. Pineapple Glazed Ham

Pineapple rings and cherries are the old-fashioned decoration, and they earn their place: the pineapple juice keeps the glaze bright and a little tangy, and the fruit caramelizes at the edges. Pin the rings on with cloves and let the ham baste in its own juices. Kids tend to love this one — the cherries disappear first.

4. Spiced Orange and Clove Ham

Orange zest, orange juice, and whole cloves give this ham a warm, spiced flavor that tastes like the holidays without being as sweet as the brown sugar versions. Stud the fat with cloves in a diamond pattern and brush the glaze on toward the end so it sets without burning. It pairs well with roasted carrots and a simple green salad.

Slow cooker and Instant Pot hams

When the oven is full of sides and desserts, these methods free it up — and they keep the ham moist, which is the whole game with a big roast.

5. Slow Cooker Christmas Ham

Set the ham in the slow cooker with the glaze and let it warm through for a few hours while you cook everything else. The slow, moist heat is forgiving, and the ham comes out tender. Finish it under the broiler for a few minutes if you want a caramelized edge — watch it closely, because sugar goes from golden to burnt fast.

6. Instant Pot Christmas Ham

The pressure cooker version is the fastest way to a glazed ham, and it is the one to reach for when time is short. The ham steams in its glaze and stays juicy; a quick finish in the oven or under the broiler gives it color. It works best with a smaller ham that fits the pot comfortably.

7. Dutch Oven Christmas Ham

A heavy Dutch oven with a tight lid does for the ham what the slow cooker does, in the oven: steady, even heat and no drying out. The glaze reduces in the bottom of the pot and makes a sauce worth spooning over the slices. This is a good method for a bone-in ham that needs a little more time.

Bold and savory glazes

For tables that want a ham with more personality — smoky, tangy, or a little boozy — these five are the ones to try.

8. Bourbon Orange Glazed Ham

Bourbon and orange are a natural pair: the bourbon adds warmth and a hint of vanilla, the orange keeps it bright. Most of the alcohol cooks off in the oven, leaving the flavor behind. This is the ham for a grown-up table, and it is excellent with roasted sweet potatoes.

9. Cranberry Glazed Ham

Cranberry sauce is not just a side dish — cooked down with a little sugar and orange, it becomes a tart, ruby-red glaze that looks beautiful on the platter. The tartness balances the salt of the ham better than any sweet glaze. Use it when the rest of the menu is rich and you want the main dish to cut through it.

10. Mustard Glazed Ham

A mustard glaze is the savory answer to the sweet glazes: sharp, tangy, and not very sweet at all. Dijon or whole-grain mustard mixed with a little brown sugar and vinegar gives the ham a crust with bite. It is the best choice if your guests prefer savory food, and it is wonderful cold the next day in sandwiches.

11. Coke-Glazed Ham

Cola-glazed ham is a Southern tradition: the cola reduces into a dark, caramel-like glaze that is sweet without tasting like candy. It sounds unusual and tastes wonderful — the caramel notes of the cola go straight at the smoky ham. Try it once and it tends to become a tradition.

12. Savory Herb-Roasted Ham

No sweet glaze at all: rosemary, thyme, garlic, and black pepper rubbed over the ham, roasted until the outside is crisp and fragrant. This is the ham for a table that already has sweet sides and desserts and needs a savory anchor. Serve it with mustard on the side and let people season their own slices.

What to serve with Christmas ham

Ham is salty and rich, so the best sides are the ones that balance it: something sweet (candied yams or roasted sweet potatoes), something green (green beans, Brussels sprouts, or a simple salad), something starchy (dinner rolls, scalloped potatoes, or mashed potatoes), and something tart (cranberry sauce or a pickled vegetable). A small bowl of good mustard on the table covers the guests who want more tang. Keep the sides simple — the ham is the star, and the table should not fight it.

What to do with leftover ham

Leftover ham is one of the best parts of Christmas dinner. Slice it thin for sandwiches with mustard and pickles, dice it into a ham and potato bake, stir it into split pea or bean soup, or fry it with eggs for breakfast the next morning. Store sliced ham in the refrigerator and use it within a few days; freeze what you cannot use, in portions, so it thaws quickly later.
